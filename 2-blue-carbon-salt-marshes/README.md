# Project 2: Blue Carbon and Nitrogen Storage in Salt Marshes (Sado Estuary)

**Question:** which plant species and plant parts store the most carbon and nitrogen, and does biomass matter?

**Data:** 14 aggregated observations from four species (*Halimione*, *Spartina*, *Salicornia halimione*, *Juncus maritimus*), each split into aboveground, dead litter and roots. Data come from a third-party doctoral research project.

**Methods:** descriptive statistics, simple linear regression (biomass vs storage), Pearson and Spearman correlation, one-way ANOVA with Tukey post hoc, Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn post hoc (Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment), Shapiro-Wilk and Levene tests (α = 5%).

**Main results**
- *Juncus maritimus* stored the most carbon (24.41 t/ha); *Salicornia halimione* the most nitrogen (0.66 t/ha).
- Biomass strongly predicted storage (R² = 0.972 for carbon, 0.894 for nitrogen), although residuals showed heteroscedasticity.
- Carbon storage differed significantly between species (ANOVA p = 0.016; Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.036).
- Carbon and nitrogen storage were strongly and positively correlated (Pearson r = 0.904).

**Limitations:** very small sample (3 to 5 observations per species); post hoc results differed between the parametric and non-parametric approaches.

**Software:** SPSS and R.
